Problem Statement · required 1025s

The world is not short of land that could capture carbon.
It is short of proof that it did.

Who has the problem

Municipalities with climate targets and no instrument. Agricultural cooperatives sitting on degraded soil. Corporates that must retire credits and cannot audit what they buy. And the family farmer, who owns the carbon and cannot reach the market.

Why today's answer fails

Certification is retrospective, slow and expensive: a first cycle typically burns 20 to 40 percent of the credit value before a single tonne is sold. The proof is built years after the land changed.

And the tooling is split in two

AI builders ship software with no domain evidence. Carbon platforms verify assets they did not originate. Nobody stands between the soil and the registry, and that gap is where the market stalls.
Put plainly: a farmer with 50 hectares of degraded soil cannot enter a market measured in billions, because proving what happened on that land costs more than the land returns.
